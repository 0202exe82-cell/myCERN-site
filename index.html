What is CERN?

CERN is the world's largest particle physics laboratory, located near Geneva, on the border between Switzerland and France. Founded in 1954, it brings together thousands of scientists from more than 100 countries to study the basic constituents of matter.

The Large Hadron Collider (LHC)

The LHC is the most powerful particle accelerator ever built. It is a 27-kilometre ring of superconducting magnets placed 100 metres underground. Protons are accelerated to almost the speed of light and then collide, recreating conditions that existed just after the Big Bang.

  • Circumference: 27 km
  • Depth: 100 m underground
  • Operating temperature: -271.3 °C (colder than outer space)
  • Key discovery: the Higgs boson (2012)

Why does it matter?

Research at CERN helps us understand dark matter, antimatter, and the origin of the universe. It has also given the world the World Wide Web, invented at CERN in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee.
